# VM timing and accelerator benchmarks

[#133](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/issues/133) tracks the longest manager VM suites. The successful [baseline CI run](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/actions/runs/38042782851) spent 746 seconds in normal-init UEFI acceptance and 761 seconds in a paired UEFI/modern/lane1 carrier/failure suite. These runs retain actual lease clocks, independent packets, device faults and desktop/keyboard checks.

The normal-init and carrier/failure harnesses accept `AXIOM64_QEMU_ACCELERATOR=tcg`, `auto` or `kvm`. Their default remains TCG. `auto` asks QEMU to initialize KVM and permits its initialization fallback to TCG; `kvm` requires KVM. QEMU's `query-kvm` response records the accelerator actually used in each result. A guest failure is a failed case, with no second emulator attempt. No guest clock or acceptance deadline changes.

The manual Build and boot workflow exposes the same `manager_accelerator` choice for measuring these two suite families. Other suites keep their current configuration. KVM access depends on the runner; the requested accelerator alone does not establish that KVM ran or improved performance. A local device-node probe could not initialize KVM, so local TCG fallback evidence cannot establish a KVM speedup.

The initial [strict-KVM run](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/actions/runs/38047203864) used actual KVM but failed three carrier cases, tracked in [#136](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/issues/136). The same-head [TCG run](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/actions/runs/38047155925) completed all 352 scoped cases. KVM completed 339 successfully, failed three and left ten unrun. The 339 jointly successful cases took 12202.141 seconds in TCG and 12788.289 seconds in KVM when summed across their individual wall times. This failed, incomplete experiment does not establish a speed improvement; TCG remains the default.

The observer now waits for both manager and independent raw-socket EIO before checking removal. The manager retains an accepted interrupted hint across fresh unconfigured retries and clears that provenance on installation, missing/invalid cache or explicit invalidation. Shutdown retires an unconfigured interrupted retry without deleting its old accepted hint. These fixes require complete TCG and KVM acceptance; any future faster policy also requires complete measured improvement.

The later [same-head TCG run](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/actions/runs/38053360735) passed all 34 gates. Its [strict-KVM counterpart](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/actions/runs/38053360959) passed the carrier suites but failed the BIOS/modern/ext2/no-server normal-init desktop before keyboard injection, tracked in [#138](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/issues/138). A single image request rejected the mapped xterm background before drawing; the failure-only screenshot subsequently showed its banner and prompt. The actual C probe reproduces this failure with delayed drawing on an owned native Xvfb; painting first passes. The probe now reads the same selected window at most twenty times, at 100 ms intervals, and still requires more than 100 different pixels. Permanently uniform images, exactly 100 different pixels and a missing window manager remain failures. `python3 scripts/tests/desktop_visual_test.py -v` covers these cases at the real probe/server seam. Complete VM acceptance remains required, including real keyboard acknowledgements in the same xterm.

The visual follow-up's [TCG run](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/actions/runs/38056484520) passed all 34 gates, with exact product checks for 352 scoped cases, four normal-init images and sixteen disks. Its [strict-KVM run](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/actions/runs/38056481964) passed all 32 normal-init cases but exposed another #136 ordering: carrier withdrawal cleared `installed` before receive EIO reached retirement, leaving accepted-hint provenance unset. A fresh unconfigured retry could then delete that hint on shutdown. Provenance now records the accepted lease at withdrawal, before its marker is cleared. `make test-manager-runtime-native` exercises the actual runtime withdrawal, retirement, fresh Client and shutdown with the real saved Store under sanitizers. Both event orderings retain accepted hints; unaccepted hints and explicit invalidation still delete them. Complete follow-up TCG and KVM acceptance remains required.

Measure focused profiles before changing the CI default. Preserve every scenario, linkage, firmware, transport, adapter lane and root type, explicit TCG coverage and all full CI gates. Publish exact sources/products and complete before/after timings. The healthy adapter's measured twenty-second T1 barrier remains required. #107 retains its separate keyboard diagnosis requirements.

The next [strict-KVM run](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/actions/runs/38059239922) exposed an observer failure in UEFI/modern/ext2/no-server, tracked in [#140](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/issues/140). The completed log shows the required 1,000 ms restart interval, but a serial read ending partway through the timestamp could satisfy the parser's end-of-buffer anchor and fail the unchanged interval assertion. Spawn, reap and observer records now require their terminating newline. `python3 scripts/tests/normal_manager_events_test.py -v` checks every byte boundary of the captured restart and all three event types with LF and CRLF; it fails before the fix and passes afterwards. No clock, backoff bound or keyboard timing changes. Complete final acceptance remains required.

The separately measured [normal-init fixture compression work](ci-artifact-compression.md) retains every image and saved disk while reducing packaging CPU. Its archive size and hosted upload timing are separate from VM accelerator measurements.

The [complete TCP profile matrix](ci-tcp-profiles.md) distributes the six independent wire profiles across isolated jobs while retaining their original commands, waits and evidence. Its hosted comparison measures the serial TCP critical path separately from setup, queue time and the remaining manager suites.

The available mechanisms follow [QEMU's accelerator documentation](https://www.qemu.org/docs/master/system/qemu-manpage.html) and [GitHub's hosted-runner KVM guidance](https://github.blog/changelog/2024-04-02-github-actions-hardware-accelerated-android-virtualization-now-available/). This work concerns VM development infrastructure; HP work still follows the required VM prerequisites.
