# Controlled TCP zero-window acceptance

[Issue #144](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/issues/144) covers a stopped receiver that continues answering TCP probes, then reopens its window. It retains the loss and receive-ordering requirements in [TCP loss acceptance](tcp-loss.md) and [receive-ordering acceptance](tcp-reordering.md). Every active/passive flow still sends and receives exactly 65,536 bytes, half-closes, reaches EOF and crosses the peer's wrapped sequence space.

The independent peer first recovers the initial data loss with a 536-byte advertised window. It accepts one further clean 536-byte segment, providing a fresh RTT sample, then advertises zero at the cumulative ACK for byte 1072. It rejects further application progress, answers two probes with the same ACK and zero window, and reopens to 8192 bytes after the third probe. Native Linux sends an empty probe at `SND.UNA - 1`; the guest sends one queued byte at `SND.UNA`. The receiver does not accept that byte until it has reopened.

A newly closed window previously reused an expired persist deadline, producing an immediate first probe. The connection now starts that period from the validated window transition and current retransmission timeout, including the RTT sample carried by its ACK. Repeated zero-window ACKs preserve exponential backoff. The sanitizer regression covers both clean and retransmitted data, exact first-probe boundaries, repeated zero ACKs, backoff and recovery of every queued byte. Initial queued data also starts with the current RTO rather than an unrelated fixed interval.

`make test-tcp-persist-native` runs glibc, static musl and dynamic musl in separate private network namespaces with independent zero-drop packet monitoring. `make test-tcp-persist` runs static/dynamic guests over BIOS/UEFI and modern/legacy virtio transports; every VM also includes e1000. The same C application is used by all three wire profiles. No existing loss or ordering target is replaced.

`python3 scripts/tcp_persist_capture.py` independently checks all 22 captures and 44 flows. It checks sequence numbers, windows, fixed ACKs, all three probes, both zero-window replies, bounded exponential elapsed intervals on PCAP and peer clocks, and exact recovery. Native first probes must wait at least 180 ms; guest probes at least 850 ms, retaining tolerance around their respective 200 ms and one-second RTO floors. The next intervals must increase; flat, immediate or unbounded retry patterns fail.

The damaged-capture regression synchronizes packet and endpoint records, repairs checksums, and requires the earlier loss/order verifier to keep passing. Its eight classes remove a probe, a zero-window reply or the reopening announcement; replace the closed window; acknowledge a byte prematurely; corrupt a probe sequence; falsify the first elapsed wait; and flatten later backoff. The full matrix requires 176 rejections. Sources, programs, six boot images, logs, captures and verified results are published by CI, and every full job must pass before merge.

[Issue #145](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/issues/145) covers receipt timing exposed by this stricter comparison. Native PCAP records use Linux's actual `SO_TIMESTAMPNS_NEW` packet-receipt timestamps, including when the collector reads queued frames late. Missing or truncated timestamp metadata fails the capture. The regression uses real AF_PACKET traffic, checks forward/backward adjustments to the collector's wall-clock reading and a paused collector, and preserves the earlier exact 1000-packet and forced-overflow cases. Kernel receipt timestamps use system time; the independent peer's monotonic intervals remain required, so inconsistent clock evidence fails rather than being rewritten. No host clock or time service is changed. The timestamp interface follows the [Linux kernel documentation](https://www.kernel.org/doc/html/latest/networking/timestamping.html).

The protocol follows [RFC 9293 section 3.8.6.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9293.html#section-3.8.6.1), with native behavior compared against [Linux's probe timer](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/3857c2fe5449541e24afc5efdb0f81a8a8f9a3a0/net/ipv4/tcp_timer.c) and [probe output](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/3857c2fe5449541e24afc5efdb0f81a8a8f9a3a0/net/ipv4/tcp_output.c). [Configured user-timeout evidence](tcp-user-timeout.md) adds separate socket/deadline and disabled-timeout controls. Hostile segments, broader congestion/default deadlines and route/device/MTU/ICMP requirements in #121/#109 remain open. Complete VM workloads and HP readiness remain later acceptance goals.
