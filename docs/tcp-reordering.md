# Controlled TCP receive-ordering acceptance

[Issue #139](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/issues/139) adds a receive-ordering subset to [#121](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/issues/121) and [#109](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/issues/109). The independent Ethernet/TCP peer retains the actual handshake, data and FIN losses described in [TCP loss acceptance](tcp-loss.md). Its return stream then starts above a real receive hole, repeats that range, supplies a consistently valued partial overlap, and fills the hole across sequence wrap. The ordinary C application still requires all 65536 exact bytes in each direction, half-close and EOF.

Relative to the peer's wrapped origin, the first four payload ranges are `(536, 536)`, `(536, 536)`, `(804, 536)` and `(0, 804)`. The peer waits for actual cumulative ACKs at the original hole before advancing to the duplicate/overlap and before sending the fill. Filling the hole must promote the queued ranges to a cumulative ACK of 1340 bytes. This exercises in-order delivery from a queued range, duplicate consistency and overlapping assembly, following the sequence-space and receive-processing requirements in [RFC 9293](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9293.html#section-3.10.7.4).

`make test-tcp-reordering-native` runs the existing C application with glibc, static musl and dynamic musl in nested private network namespaces. It retains the independent capture's receive budget, zero-drop requirement, actual client status and namespace drain barrier. `make test-tcp-reordering` runs static/dynamic guests with BIOS/UEFI and modern/legacy virtio, each including virtio-net and e1000 with active and passive opens. The shared harness's default remains the original loss profile; `--wire reordering` selects the additional peer and separate report, capture and image names.

`python3 scripts/tcp_reordering_capture.py` independently checks all 22 captures and 44 streams, including the original exact bytes, loss timers, checksums, sequence wrap and final FIN acknowledgment. It also checks the actual range order, hole ACKs before peer advances, and promotion after the fill. `python3 scripts/tests/tcp_reordering_capture_test.py` rejects five additional damage classes per capture: removal of the initial queued segment, removal of its duplicate, replacement of the partial overlap by an adjacent range, acknowledgment beyond the hole, and removal of the first hole ACK. Each mutation updates matching peer evidence and repairs applicable checksums; the original loss verifier must still accept it before the ordering verifier rejects it. Short Ethernet-frame padding remains outside the IPv4/TCP checksum.

The `tcp-fault` CI job retains every original loss/native/guest/checker step and adds this complete matrix and its 110 rejection cases. It publishes both profiles' boot images and separate evidence with corresponding sources and inputs. Local acceptance passes; the exact final candidate still requires complete CI before merging or closing #139. Conflicting overlaps, hostile segments/resets, receive-window/persist behavior, broader congestion/backoff/deadlines, driver/route/MTU/ICMP failures and reclamation remain in #121/#109. VM workload readiness and HP support remain later milestones.
