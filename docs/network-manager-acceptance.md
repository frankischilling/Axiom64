All 47 required jobs pass. [PR #95](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/pull/95) merged the identical tested tree; the subsequent Linux-only catalog and desktop-harness update leaves the kernel, installed userspace and network fixture sources unchanged.

The independent audit retains all 842 earlier records and adds the complete 320-case carrier/device matrix: 71 reports and 1162 passing records. Records include host checks and paired-boot summaries as well as guest cases. The actual-process matrices cover both Ethernet drivers together, both musl linkages, BIOS/UEFI and modern/legacy virtio. Protocol and carrier matrices also exercise both affected adapter roles.

## Retry policy and limits

Discovery remains asynchronous and continues when no server answers. Its randomized exponential backoff has a 64-second base cap, with one-second jitter. REQUEST and saved-address INIT-REBOOT attempts use four accepted transmissions before abandoning the attempt and starting fresh discovery; a stale saved address is forgotten. Discovery retransmissions may retain their transaction ID, as permitted by [RFC 2131](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2131#section-4.1). There is no whole-service acquisition deadline or fallback installation of an unvalidated address.

The modules bound interfaces, packets, routes, resolver text and receive/action work as described in [configuration](network-configuration.md) and [manager integration](network-manager.md). Synchronous storage requests can each wait up to 30 seconds; the manager's event-loop shutdown budgets do not bound total wall time. Native UID/path checks operate under the documented trusted-writer policy and do not establish complete kernel credential enforcement.

These completed client/service contracts leave [#12](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/issues/12), [#13](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/issues/13), [#14](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/issues/14), [#9](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/issues/9) and [#10](https://github.com/frankischilling/Axiom64/issues/10) open. This evidence uses controlled peers and QEMU; it does not establish a general exposed-network release or the HP target.
